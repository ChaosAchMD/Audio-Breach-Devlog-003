# Audio Breach — Devlog #003: The Person Behind the Silence

In the first devlog, I asked what would happen if making a sound was the worst mistake you could make. In the second, I talked about how small details and moments of silence can make you doubt what you just saw and heard.

This time, I want to take a step back and talk about the person behind those ideas, where the ambition to make games began, and why Audio Breach is a project I finally decided to bring to life.

## It Started with Scratch

My interest in programming started with Scratch. I enjoyed creating things with it, and I always wanted to become a game developer.

That ambition stayed with me, even as the world around game development continued to change. The idea of creating something of my own, something people could actually experience, never completely disappeared.

Eventually, I decided it was time to stop thinking about it and give it a real chance.

## An Idea That Waited for Years

Audio Breach wasn't an idea I came up with overnight. The concept had been on my mind for years before I started actively working on it a few months ago.

At some point, I had to make a choice: keep imagining what the game could be, or start trying to make it real.

I chose to try.

Of course, I hope Audio Breach will become a success. But right now, the important thing is giving the idea the opportunity to become something more than a concept I've carried around in my head.

I want to create a horror experience that stays with players, not simply one that makes them jump and then forget what happened a few seconds later.

## Building the Experience

The heart of Audio Breach is its atmosphere.

I want sound, silence, and uncertainty to play a meaningful role in the experience. Instead of relying on constant jumpscares, I want to explore the tension that comes from not knowing exactly what is nearby, what a sound means, or whether the environment is telling you something you haven't noticed yet.

Sometimes, what you cannot see is more unsettling than what is directly in front of you.

Turning that idea into an actual game comes with its own challenges.

Map construction, character designs, and audio are three areas that require particular attention. Each element needs to fit the world and contribute to the experience rather than feel disconnected from it.

A map should do more than provide somewhere to walk. Character designs should feel like they belong in the same world. And audio needs to do more than fill the silence — it needs to make that silence meaningful.

Getting everything to work together is something I'm still working through. Having an idea of how something should feel is one thing; finding a way to make that feeling work inside a game is another.

That process involves experimenting, testing, making changes, and figuring out what works best for the experience I'm trying to create.

As I wrote in the first devlog:

> One of the things I enjoy most about developing this project is being able to build an idea, test it, change it and see it become something more unsettling than I first imagined.

That is still an important part of the journey.

## Where KINETIX Comes In

Audio Breach is part of KINETIX Core Technologies, the broader initiative behind my KINETIX projects.

My ambition goes beyond simply having an idea and leaving it unfinished. I want to build projects that people notice, take seriously, and genuinely want to experience.

For Audio Breach and KINETIX, the goal is success and recognition. I know neither is guaranteed, and I don't want to make promises about outcomes or dates that I cannot guarantee.

What I can do is keep working towards that ambition and give the projects the opportunity to grow.

Audio Breach is one part of that bigger picture, and I want to see how far I can take it.

## Still Building

Audio Breach is still in development, and there is more to figure out before I can share the experience I ultimately want players to have.

I don't have a major announcement or release date to share in this devlog. Instead, I wanted to give a little more context about the person behind the project and the ambition that keeps it moving forward.

The direction remains the same: a horror experience built around atmosphere, sound, silence, and the uncertainty of what might be waiting just beyond what you can see.

There is still work to do, and I'm looking forward to continuing the process of turning this idea into something real.

Sometimes, the scariest thing isn't what you see.

**One question before I go:**

*What makes a horror game genuinely unsettling for you — what you see, what you hear, or what your imagination fills in when you don't know what's there?*

I'd love to hear your thoughts.

Until the next update…

**Stay quiet. Listen carefully.**

— The Audio Breach Developer
